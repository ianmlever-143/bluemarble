    About

    How to play

    Every week there are three new Yids: a Today Yid, a Yester Yid and a Really Old Yid. Everyone gets the same three. Missed a week? Find it under Past weeks.

    Each one comes with three clues. Name them on the first clue for 54 points, the second for 36, the third for 18. Start typing a name and pick from the list, or type your own. A wrong guess flips the next clue. Take all the time you want.

    Then the bonus: the three share a connection to one another that you have to figure out. We call it the thread, and guessing it is worth 18 more. Stuck after one guess? Take a hint and it's worth 9. A perfect score is 180, which is ten times chai.

    Play with friends. Start a group, send your friends the invite link, and see how you stack up every week.

    Why's it called "Geshmak!"?

    Geshmak (guh-SHMAHK) is Yiddish for delicious, delightful, a pleasure. It shares a root with the German Geschmack, "taste." You can eat something geshmak, and you can also do something with a geshmak, meaning with real relish.

    For generations the famous Yiddish sigh was "S'iz shver tsu zayn a Yid": it's hard to be a Jew. Sholem Aleichem even made it the title of a play.

    The comeback, a favorite in Hasidic teaching, flips one word: "S'iz geshmak tsu zayn a Yid." It's delicious, or awesome to be Jewish!

    And Yid? As you might know, it's the Yiddish word for a Jew. Here it's not said as a slur, but reclaimed as a symbol of pride in our past, present, and future.
